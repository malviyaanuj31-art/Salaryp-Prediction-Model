5 How Does This Work? (Beginner's Guide)

Linear Regression learns the relationship between years of experience and salary from historical data. It finds the best-fit straight line through the data points and uses this line to estimate a salary for any given number of years of experience.

Feature (X)
The input variable the model uses to make a prediction. Here it is Years of Experience.
Target (y)
The output variable the model tries to predict. Here it is Salary.
Training Data
The portion of the dataset (80%) used to teach the model by showing it many (Experience, Salary) examples.
Testing Data
The remaining 20% of data that the model has never seen, used to measure how well it generalises to new examples.
Regression Coefficient
The slope of the regression line – it tells us how much the salary increases for every additional year of experience.
Intercept
The predicted salary when years of experience is zero – the point where the regression line crosses the y-axis.
R² Score
Tells us what percentage of the variation in salary is explained by years of experience. Closer to 1.0 is better.
MAE / RMSE
Average difference between actual and predicted salaries. Lower values indicate a more accurate model.
